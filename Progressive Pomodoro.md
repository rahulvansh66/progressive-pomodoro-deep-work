# Progressive Pomodoro

## TL;DR

- OG Pomo's fixed 25-minute block is often too long to start and too short to stay in flow.
- Progressive Pomo: start with a tiny block, rate your focus, skip the break if you're flowing, then adjust the next block's length.
- Goal: actively cultivate flow, which builds gradually.

## &#32;the problem with Pomodoro

- Hook: many people try Pomodoro and still don't make the progress they expected.
- Pitch: Pomodoro has flaws but can be improved, and the fix is "Progressive Pomodoro."
- Terms: the original is called "OG Pomo" and the new version "Progressive Pomo."
- What people really want is to get things done and feel accomplished, which means getting into **flow**. OG Pomo doesn't do the best job of getting you there.

## Limitations of OG Pomo

- OG Pomo recap: alternate 25 minutes of focus with a 5-minute break, and after every fourth cycle take a longer 15–30 minute break.
- Main benefit: simplicity, and it's a decent way to get into focus.
- Core argument: the alleged strength, the fixed 25-minute block, is its biggest flaw.
- **Too long:** 25 minutes can feel unbearable, which feeds procrastination while you wait to feel mentally ready. Bad starting moments include after waking, after a heavy meal, Mondays, a caffeine crash, and context switching.
- Focus is not an on/off switch. It builds gradually (the video cites Andrew Huberman) and is not a step function from unfocused to maximum focus.
- **Too short:** if you catch flow and the timer rings, OG Pomo says to stop and break, which kills momentum.
- Flow is where the best work happens and it feels great, so it's the state to optimize for.
- Main goal of Progressive Pomo: **actively cultivate the flow state**.

## The Progressive Pomo steps

- Since flow builds gradually, you need a system that builds up gradually too.
- Accept that you'll move in and out of varying levels of focus. This takes the pressure off feeling focused all the time.

1. **Start small and get moving.** Pick a block length proportional to your current focus and motivation, for example 5 minutes at the start of the day. A small commitment lowers the barrier to starting, so the only mission is to begin.
2. **Rate your focus after each block.** Use four ratings: *distracted*, *okay*, *highly focused*, *flow*. The rating is the "secret sauce," because it decides whether you take a break.
3. **Break or accept.** OG Pomo always breaks. In Progressive Pomo, if you're flowing you skip the break ("if you're flowing, keep on flowing"), and if not, you're free to take one.
4. **Adjust.** Before the next block, increase, decrease or keep the length based on your rating. If momentum is building, increase it, for example 5 to 10 or 10 to 15 minutes.

- Summary: build motivation and focus iteratively, and be okay with starting small.

## Example progression

- Start the day with a 5-minute block. Afterward you feel a little distracted.
- Choose a shorter 3-minute block with deeper intent. Afterward you're slightly more focused.
- After a short break, increase to 10 minutes. Afterward you feel highly focused.
- After another break, stretch to 20 minutes. You barely notice the time because you've caught flow.
- The timer goes off at 20 minutes and you keep going: "if you're flowing, keep on flowing."
- Main idea: start small, grow your blocks until you reach flow, then stay there as long as you can.

## Benefits and action items

**Benefits over OG Pomo:**

- It's adaptable and flexible.
- It reduces procrastination by lowering the barrier to entry.
- It actively cultivates the flow state.

**Action items:**

1. **Keep the setup simple.** All you need is a timer and the habit of rating your focus after each block.
2. **Keep ratings honest.** It's fine to be distracted, and the system accommodates that. Expect to hop between distracted → highly focused and okay → flow.
3. **Start small.** The speaker usually starts at 5 minutes, and drops to 2 minutes when feeling lethargic or unmotivated.

- Rule of thumb: the more resistance you feel to starting, the shorter your focus block should be.
- If you're used to 25-minute blocks this may feel challenging, but the small change makes a big difference.

## Quick reference

- **Start:** 2–5 minutes, scaled to your resistance.
- **After each block:** rate yourself as distracted, okay, highly focused, or flow.
- **If flowing:** skip the break and keep going.
- **If not flowing:** take a break.
- **Next block:** increase, decrease or keep the length based on the rating.
